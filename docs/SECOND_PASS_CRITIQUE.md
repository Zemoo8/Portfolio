# Second pass — critique of what was actually built

Walkthrough of the live site (desktop 1440 × 900 and phone 390 × 844, real Chrome), 2026-09-26.
Contact sheets: [`second-pass/before-desktop.jpg`](second-pass/before-desktop.jpg) · [`second-pass/before-mobile.jpg`](second-pass/before-mobile.jpg).

The site is coherent and technically clean. It is also a template. Below, without softening.

## 1. What looks generic

- **The section formula.** About, Path, Credentials, Capabilities, More work and Contact are the same slide: a small dash-label (`— ABOUT`), a big serif headline, a paragraph, content. Six times. On the phone it is literally the same screen with different words.
- **The parchment panel** with a saffron line on its top edge wraps every section. It stops meaning anything by the second one.
- **The case studies** are one layout filled six times: back link, label, title, brush underline, italic tagline, a three-cell meta table, a matted film. AI Eyes, a bakery, a missile-tracking simulation and a video game look identical.
- **The hero**: label, name, paragraph, two buttons, a picture on the right. The standard landing page, dressed in ink.
- Headline copy like "Six builds, carried by one current." and "Let's build something that matters." is portfolio boilerplate.

## 2. What looks AI-generated

- **The orange sun.** The painted backdrop is fixed, so the same salmon circle sits in the same spot on every screen: behind About, behind Path, behind Credentials, behind Contact. It is the "weird decorative point", site-wide.
- **The painted mountains as wallpaper** behind everything. A beautiful painting used as a screensaver.
- A **brush underline under every title** — the same ornament, stamped.
- **System vocabulary**: numbered chapters (`01 Work`, `02 About`…), `01 / 06` counters, `01 · ASSISTIVE AI · MOBILE`, GPS coordinates under the portrait. It reads as "developer portfolio" before anything is read.
- The **air emblem** at the centre of the orbit is fine as the orbit's idea; everywhere else "a symbol" would be branding for its own sake.

## 3. Decoration rather than design

The sun, the mountains behind text sections, the saffron ribbons, the brush underline under every heading, the torn paper (already removed), the ensō ring around the portrait (a frame around a photograph that is already a circle), the coordinates caption, the italic outlined numeral behind the orbit caption.

## 4. Sections that look too similar

About / Path / Credentials / Capabilities / More work / Contact (same formula, same panel, same background). All six case studies. Hero and About both put a painted ring around a picture.

## 5. Where surprise is missing

Everywhere after the orbit. The page never changes colour, scale or rhythm again. Nothing is ever larger than expected, nothing bleeds off the edge, nothing breaks the grid, no photograph ever fills the screen, the work is never bigger than a card.

## 6. Where the eye has nowhere interesting to go

Path (two text columns on paper), Capabilities (a numbered list), More work (a list with grey thumbnails), Contact (a headline over wallpaper), the top of every case study (a meta table).

## 7. Where typography could be far more expressive

- The name is the only real typographic moment, and it is the conventional two-line stack.
- Path is chronological, yet its years are 11 px labels — the years *are* the structure and should be the architecture.
- Credentials has a number (18) hidden in a sentence.
- Contact: the email address is the only thing that matters and it is set smaller than the headline above it.
- Nothing ever uses the full width of the viewport.

## 8. Where project media could become the composition

Every film sits in a mat inside a column. The AI Eyes ad is vertical — it wants to be a full-height column of the screen. The Cheezy film is a product campaign — it wants a campaign spread. The AEGIS radar is a scientific instrument — it wants a grid and its equation. Books is data — its real scraped titles and prices could be the texture. SubwayRunner is a 3D game — it wants perspective.

## 9. Interactions that are impressive

- The orbit around the air element (now calm: rest, glide, rest), with the smoke vortex centred on it.
- The certificate rail with the real documents and the lightbox.
- The page change painted in through the brush.

## 10. Interactions that are just effects

Headings rising out of blur; the unrolling parchment; the ensō painting itself on every section; the breathing mist in the moon gate; the brush underline sliding in under links. Pleasant, weightless — none of them tells the visitor anything.

---

## What the second pass does about it

- **One idea per scene, a different rhythm for each.** Hero (typographic), intro (one circle and silence), Work (immersive: the orbit is the index, then a world per project), About (photographic), Path (the years are the architecture), Credentials (an archive), Capabilities (information design: which tool built which project), Contact (a last, quiet, dark scene).
- **The painting becomes a scene, not a wallpaper.** No fixed backdrop, no sun, no parchment panels. Paper, ink and the work, with real contrast between light and dark scenes.
- **The name is the hero.** The photograph becomes the *o* in *Baghouli* — the circle comes from the photograph, not from a frame. It opens the intro film.
- **Navigation stops being a bar.** Two corners: the name, and the chapter you are in — which opens a full-screen index with previews.
- **Projects get their own worlds**, built from their own media: vision (AI Eyes), campaign (Cheezy), science (AEGIS), data (Books), energy (Sandy), space (SubwayRunner). The case studies open with the same world.
- **Less system**: no chapter numbers, no counters, no coordinates.
- Three moments worth remembering — chosen for what they do, not how they look: the portrait-*o* that opens the film; an orbit card that takes over the screen and lands you in its project's world; the matrix where hovering a tool lights up every project it built.

## Result

- **Hero experiments** (three built, one chosen): [`second-pass/hero-experiments.jpg`](second-pass/hero-experiments.jpg). 1 — the photograph as the *o* of *Baghouli* (chosen: it reads instantly, it only works because it is his face and his name, and it survives as a static image). 2 — a statement wrapping around a large circular portrait (reads as a magazine article). 3 — a painted landscape with the face as the moon (two circles competing with the painted sun; the least personal).
- **Before / after**, same ten views: [`second-pass/before-after.jpg`](second-pass/before-after.jpg).
- The three moments: the *o* that lifts out of the name and becomes the introduction circle as you scroll; a project card that takes over the screen and lands you inside its world; the capabilities field where pointing at a project leaves only the tools that built it.
- Removed: the fixed painted backdrop and its sun, the parchment panels, the seal, the brush ring around the portrait, numbered navigation, counters, coordinates, the floating certificates, the breathing mist.
